IELTS Speaking Part 1: Introduction and Interview

Duration: 4 to 5 minutes. Direct questions about yourself, your daily life, routine, and personal experiences.

Overview: The examiner asks direct questions about familiar topics. This is typically the most natural and conversational part of the test.

Common Themes:

  • Work or Studies: What you do, why you chose your profession/course, future plans.
  • Home & Housing: What your home/apartment is like, your neighborhood, how long you have lived there.
  • Hometown: What your city is famous for, what there is to do, whether you enjoy living there.
  • Interests & Hobbies: Free time activities, sports, reading, music, social media habits.
  • Daily Routines: Daily schedule, eating habits, travel, and holidays.

IELTS Speaking Part 2: Individual Long Turn (Cue Card)

Duration: 3 to 4 minutes (1 min prep, 1-2 mins speaking). Talk on a specific topic without interruption.

Overview: The examiner gives you a task card with a specific topic and prompts. You have 1 minute to prepare and make notes on paper, then speak continuously for 1 to 2 minutes.

Common Cue Card Themes:

  • Events & Experiences: "Describe a time when you helped someone" or "Describe a memorable trip you took."
  • People: "Describe a teacher who greatly influenced you" or "Describe a family member you resemble."
  • Objects & Places: "Describe a piece of technology you use frequently" or "Describe a park or garden you enjoy visiting."
  • Habits & Skills: "Describe a skill you learned recently" or "Describe an interesting book you read."

IELTS Speaking Part 3: Two-way Discussion

Duration: 4 to 5 minutes. Abstract, in-depth discussion connected to Part 2 themes.

Overview: The examiner asks deeper, more abstract follow-up questions linked to Part 2. Sophisticated language is required to argue, analyze pros and cons, speculate, and defend viewpoints.

Common Themes (Socio-cultural & Technological):

  • Education & Learning: Shifts in teaching methods, the future of schools, AI in education.
  • Technology & Society: Social media impact on communication, screen addiction, automation and the future of work.
  • Environment: Climate change, individual vs. governmental responsibility in sustainability.
  • Culture & Tradition: Globalization effects on local cultures, preserving historical heritage.
  • Work & Lifestyle: Work-life balance, stress in major cities.

Academic Tip: While Parts 1 & 2 are personal, Part 3 requires formal vocabulary, complex grammatical structures (passive voice, conditionals, subordinate clauses), and advanced cohesive devices to hit B2–C1 proficiency levels.
