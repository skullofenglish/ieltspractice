IELTS Assessment & General Notes

How IELTS is Assessed + General Notes and Tips[cite: 6]
Listening
About: 30 minutes. 4 recorded monologues/conversations with 40 questions. Parts 1 and 2 relate to everyday situations; Parts 3 and 4 relate to educational and training situations[cite: 6].
What is assessed:
  • Capability to identify main ideas and recognise opinions and attitudes[cite: 6].
  • Understanding of the purpose behind what is being said[cite: 6].
  • Following how ideas develop throughout a conversation[cite: 6].
Tips:
  • Think about the context before listening[cite: 6].
  • Read the questions first[cite: 6].
  • Identify from the questions, the type of information you need to listen for[cite: 6].
Reading
About: 60 minutes. 3 long reading passages with respective tasks (40 questions). Texts range from descriptive and factual to discursive and analytical. Includes non-verbal material (maps, diagrams, graphs, etc.). Authentic texts from books, journals, newspapers, magazines, etc[cite: 6].
What is assessed:
  • General understanding[cite: 6]
  • Identifying main ideas[cite: 6]
  • Identifying specific details[cite: 6]
  • Skimming and understanding logical arguments[cite: 6]
  • Identifying inferences and implied meaning[cite: 6]
  • Recognising opinions, attitudes and purpose[cite: 6]
Tips:
  • Analyse questions carefully and identify what information is needed[cite: 6].
  • Use skimming and scanning depending on the type of information you need to find[cite: 6].
Writing
About: 60 minutes. Task 1 is for describing visual information in minimum 150 words. Task 2 is for presenting a point of view, argument or problem to discuss in at least 250 words[cite: 6].
What is assessed:
  • Academic or formal style[cite: 6]
  • Sentences written in full (DO NOT use brief sentences, bullet points, or note style)[cite: 6]
  • Task achievement (how you expressed what the task requires)[cite: 6]
  • Coherence and cohesion[cite: 6]
  • Lexical resource (vocabulary)[cite: 6]
  • Grammatical range and accuracy[cite: 6]
Tips:
  • Analyse questions carefully and plan out what you will write before you start writing[cite: 6].
  • Focus on purpose and structure your text logically and clearly[cite: 6].
  • Decide on your position before writing[cite: 6].
  • Use examples and facts from the text to support and justify your arguments[cite: 6].
Speaking
About: 11-14 minutes. Interview format with an examiner in 3 parts. Part 1 is about you, familiar topics (home, family, work, study, etc). Parts 2 and 3 discuss an opinion at length[cite: 6].
What is assessed:
  • Ability to create meaningful interactions in a range of topics[cite: 6]
  • Fluency and coherence[cite: 6]
  • Lexical resource (vocabulary)[cite: 6]
  • Grammatical range and accuracy[cite: 6]
  • Pronunciation[cite: 6]
Tips:
  • Listen carefully to the questions[cite: 6].
  • Use fillers and hesitation devices when you need time to pause and think before answering[cite: 6].
  • Focus on your language, not your opinion[cite: 6].
